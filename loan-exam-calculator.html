    Amortization schedule

    Why the balance only goes down

    The balance column is the principal balance. Chapter 15 loans are simple interest: interest is figured only on unpaid principal, never added on and never compounded (§ 6.2-1520 A). Interest builds up on the side and is paid off by the next payment, so principal can only fall.

    If a payment doesn't cover the interest accrued, the shortfall stays as unpaid interest. It still isn't added to the balance.

    "Principal only" rows

    Money applied 100% to principal, skipping accrued interest. The interest isn't lost; it carries forward and the next regular payment collects it, which is why that next payment shows more interest than usual.

    Common sources: extra payments the borrower designated, processing-fee rebates (§ 6.2-1520 C), refunds of excess charges credited to the account (§ 6.2-1542 A), or insurance refunds. Trace each to support.

    Why small differences show up

    On $2,000 at 36%, a 360-day year adds about $0.82 a month over a 365-day year, and counting one extra day adds about $1.97 a period. Over a year those become the "$8 for no reason" differences.

    Use Find matching settings first. Whatever is left after the conventions match is a real difference in the data. Note: an extra day per period isn't "days elapsed," so the statutory recompute never uses it.

    How the calculator works

    • As posted (math check): recomputes every transaction exactly as posted, using your convention settings. Differences from the system's figures are calculation accuracy issues.
    • Statutory limits: same history, but always uses days elapsed, caps the rate at 36%, drops unsupported late fees (no installment over 10 days past due, or already charged), caps late/returned-check/processing fees, applies 6% after maturity + 6 months, death + 90 days, or bankruptcy + 90 days, and the judgment rate after judgment.
    • Reversals: a reversal is matched to the latest earlier payment of the same amount, and that payment is treated as never received.
    • Late-fee test: an installment is satisfied when cumulative regular payments received by the due date + grace days cover cumulative scheduled payments. Principal-only payments don't count toward installments.
    • Excess collected = interest + fees collected per the system (or the as-posted recompute if system figures are blank) minus interest + fees collectible under the statutory recompute.
